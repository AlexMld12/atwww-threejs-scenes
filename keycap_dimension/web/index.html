Eight parts, held to tolerances you will never see, so that pressing one key becomes a signal. You feel none of them — only the sound they leave behind.

Know more[ About ] us

SUBKEY is a study of the smallest machine most of us touch every day. We took a Keychron K2 apart, key by key and switch by switch, and rebuilt it as a scroll: eighty-four keycaps lift, eighty-four switches follow, and one of them opens up into the eight parts that turn a press into a signal. None of it is decoration: every piece is modelled on the real thing, lit and baked in Blender, then brought into the browser so you can take it apart.

It was designed and built by ATWWW, a small studio working where design meets code. We care about the parts nobody sees — the tolerances, the timing, the few milliseconds between a key going down and a letter showing up on screen. This project is our way of slowing that moment down long enough to look at it.

Every keystroke is a tiny mechanical event: a spring gives, two leaves of brass touch, a circuit closes. You do it thousands of times a day without thinking. We just wanted you to hear it once.
